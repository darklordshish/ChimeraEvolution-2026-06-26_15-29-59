# Industry practice: one unified rig system for many species (canonical skeletons, templates, cross-species retarget/morph)

Research date: 2026-09-29. Tool calls ~15. Primary sources preferred; uncertainty marked.

## Q1. Which studios/tools use a canonical/template skeleton with semantic joint labels mapped per species, and how do they handle extra/missing parts?

### Takeaway
Industry does NOT use one skeleton for all species. It uses (a) a small set of **archetype skeletons** per body plan (Monster Hunter, No Man's Sky, Rigify's metarig set), each species being a variant/re-proportioning of one archetype, and (b) for truly open morphology (Spore) a **semantic tag layer on top of an arbitrary graph**: bodies carry capability tags (spine / grasper / foot / mouth…), and higher-level structures (legs, shoulders/hips) are *derived by graph queries*, not stored. Extra/missing parts are handled by "unmapped = unconstrained" or by derived fallbacks (e.g. Spore turns spine bodies into pseudo-feet for legless creatures).

### Cited Findings
- **Spore (EA/Maxis, SIGGRAPH 2008, Hecker et al.)**: a character is a graph of "bodies" (20–80 per creature) that carry the usual bone data plus **tags called capabilities ("caps")** describing semantics to the animation system, e.g. the body with a hand mesh has the *grasper* cap. Bodies form a DAG with **a serial chain of spine bodies at the root**; the root body is a unique spine body chosen **by heuristic (max number of incident leg limbs + position)**. The rest pose is not a standard pose — "we cannot count on any specific configuration". — [Hecker et al. 2008, SIGGRAPH paper PDF](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Spore derives structure by query, not by stored labels: a **leg** is "a path through the tree of connected limb segments with a foot body at the leaf and a spine segment at the base (the hip)"; a **limb modifier "SpineSegment"** walks up from a selected body to the first spine body, "effectively finding an approximation to the clavicle/shoulder (or hip/pelvis)". Selection also uses **spatial queries** (front/back half-space, relative to the bounding box of the selected set). — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Spore missing parts: "A heuristic is used to determine if a character with no feet should float above the ground or crawl… Crawling characters have **some spine bodies converted into pseudo-feet** and an inch-worm-like gait". Branching predicates like `UprightSpine`, `HasGraspers`, `HasFeet` choose animation variants (e.g. use mouth when no graspers). — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Spore GDC 2007 framing of the problem: "my creature might have two arms and one leg, yours might have no arms and seven legs, and two mouths." — [Hecker, GDC 2007 page](https://www.chrishecker.com/How_To_Animate_a_Character_You've_Never_Seen_Before)
- **No Man's Sky (Hello Games, ~2016)**: art director Grant Duncan: "it's quite surprising how few different types of skeletons there are"; creatures come from a few **blueprints** (archetypes) which are then procedurally distorted (height, weight, proportions, bone thickness change together). Secondary/press source, not a technical talk; details of skeleton format not public. — [Rambus blog summary](https://www.rambus.com/blogs/the-algorithms-of-no-mans-sky-2/); [Game Developer article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/what-the-code-of-i-no-man-s-sky-i-says-about-procedural-generation)
- **Monster Hunter: World (Capcom, 2018)**: monsters share a few skeleton families — e.g. Barroth/Radobaan/Uragaan share one skeleton and head-smash attacks; Pukei-Pukei and Paolumu share the Rathalos-family skeleton. New skeleton types are expensive, shared ones cheap. Source is community/Wikipedia-level, not Capcom technical documentation (uncertainty: medium). — [Wikipedia: Monster Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Hunter); [MHW modding wiki](https://github.com/Ezekial711/MonsterHunterWorldModding/wiki/Working-with-CATS-Rigs)
- **Blender Rigify** ships a **set of metarigs** (human, basic quadruped, cat, wolf, horse, bird, shark); every rig component ("rig type": spine, limb, tail, fins…) can be combined freely to build species without a template. So the "canonical" unit is the **rig component type (chain semantics)**, not a whole skeleton. — [Rigify 0.5 wiki](https://github.com/eigen-value/rigify/wiki/2.-Rigify-0.5); [CGDive Rigify ch.3](https://cgdive.com/rig-anything-with-rigify-chapter-3-the-prebuilt-metarigs-human-and-quadruped/)
- **Unity Mecanim Humanoid**: a fixed canonical humanoid with **required bones (min. 15) and optional bones**; more optional bones mapped → better retarget quality. Non-humanoids fall into "Generic", where the skeleton is arbitrary and only a root node is specified (no cross-skeleton retarget). — [Unity Manual: Retargeting](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/Retargeting.html); [Unity Manual: Using Humanoid Characters](https://docs.unity3d.com/510/Documentation/Manual/UsingHumanoidChars.html)
- **Daz Genesis (8/9)**: one base figure/skeleton for all characters; shape morphs that move volume also move **joint centers** via "Adjust Rigging to Shape", linked to the morph slider by "ERC Freeze" (0% = default joints, 100% = moved joints) — i.e. skeleton positions are a **linear function of morph weight**. — [Daz docs: Tweaking a Figure Skeleton](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/software/dazstudio/4/userguide/creating_content/modeling/tutorials/auto_rig/start); [Versluis 2021, ERC Freeze](https://www.versluis.com/2021/02/daz-erc-freeze/)
- **Research canonical animal models**: SMAL (CVPR 2017) — one template mesh with **33 parts** in T-pose and a learned shape space covering lions, cats, dogs, horses, cows, hippos, from 41 toy scans; generalizes to unseen quadrupeds. — [Zuffi et al. 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Zuffi_3D_Menagerie_Modeling_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf); [SMAL site](https://smal.is.tue.mpg.de/)
- Reveret et al. (SCA 2005) "Morphable model of quadruped skeletons": a single morphable 3D skeleton for four-footed animals built by PCA over expert-made reference skeletons; quaternion rotations in a **global frame**; **3 components capture 90% of variance**, yielding 3 intuitive parameters measurable on any quadruped mesh. — [Reveret project page](http://morpheo.inrialpes.fr/people/reveret/MorphableSkeleton/index.html); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/1073368.1073386)

### Inferences
- Two proven shapes of "unified": **same topology, different numbers** (SMAL, Reveret, Daz, Monster Hunter families, NMS blueprints) and **different topology, shared semantics** (Spore caps, Rigify component types, Unreal chains). A cross-body-plan system (biped↔quadruped↔snake) in practice always ends up in the second shape.
- Spore's key design choice worth copying: store few tags on nodes (spine/foot/grasper/mouth…), **compute** legs, hips, shoulders by graph walks. Shoulders/hips are "first spine body above a limb", not a stored label.
- Helper/muscle-bulge bones are nowhere part of the canonical layer: Mecanim ignores them (optional/unmapped), KineFX leaves them unconstrained, Unreal chains cover "the whole chain regardless of bone count". They are treated as payload of a segment, not as correspondence points.

### Gaps
- No primary technical source (GDC talk/paper) found for No Man's Sky's skeleton data format or Monster Hunter's internal skeleton sharing; only interviews/community.
- Ark, Spore's later stages, and other "many species" AAA titles (e.g. Planet Zoo) — no primary rig-pipeline source found in this pass.

## Q2. How do retargeting systems define correspondence between skeletons of different topology?

### Takeaway
All mainstream systems converge on **chains + a few landmark joints**: correspondence is declared between *named chains* (spine, neck, left arm, left leg, tail…) plus a root/pelvis; inside a chain, bones are matched by **normalized arc-length parameter** (0 at chain start, 1 at end), so bone counts may differ. Unmapped joints are left free. Learning-based methods require skeletons to be **homeomorphic** (reducible to a common "primal" skeleton by merging chain joints).

### Cited Findings
- **Unreal IK Rig / IK Retargeter (UE5, docs current for 5.8)**: retargeting is defined "by joint chains rather than individual bones… if your target character contains more arm joints than your source, the retargeting behavior still works correctly because you are defining the entire arm chain." — [Epic: Animation Retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine); [Epic: IK Rig retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)
- Unreal FK chain **Interpolated** mode: "The source and target chains are normalized by their length, then each target bone rotation is generated by finding the rotation at the same normalized distance on the source chain" (best for different bone counts/proportions). Alternatives: One-to-One (by order), One-to-One Reversed (from chain end). Chain mapping by name: "Map All (Fuzzy)" / "Map All (Exact)". Pelvis is the retarget root; IK goals at chain ends; "Scale Source", pole-vector alignment, stride warping handle proportion differences. — [Epic: Retargeting Operation Stack 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/retargeting-operation-stack-in-unreal-engine-5-8)
- **Houdini KineFX**: `Rig Match Pose` conforms rest poses (bounding-box scale/translate + manual pose), `Map Points` stores source→target joint pairs — **sparse mapping** (root, hips, shoulders, ankles, head) for Physical Full Body IK or **dense mapping** (also knees, elbows, spine) for FABRIK; `Full Body IK` then solves the target from offsets. Unmatched joints are simply not constrained. SideFX later added a "Skeleton Swap" approach for wildly different skeletons (forum sneak preview; status uncertain). — [SideFX: Transferring animation](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/character/kinefx/retargeting.html); [SideFX forum: Skeleton Swap](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/90694/)
- **Auto-Rig Pro Remap (Blender)**: a bone mapping list (source→target by name + rest orientation), optional IK for feet/hands with pole settings and an interactive offset for different proportions; 3-bone IK option for digitigrade quadrupeds. — [Auto-Rig Pro Remap docs](https://www.lucky3d.fr/auto-rig-pro/doc/remap_doc.html)
- **Unity Mecanim**: fixed humanoid schema of named slots (required/optional); muscle-space normalization; generic rigs are not retargetable across skeletons. — [Unity Manual: Retargeting](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/Retargeting.html)
- **Spore** instead of joint correspondence uses **movement modes = coordinate frames** in which a pose is recorded: generalization `qg = G(b, qs, m)` and specialization `qs = S(b, qg, m)` per body. Modes include scaling by character size and **LimbLength** (limb length = path length from body to nearest spine segment → large motions on long limbs, small on short). A two-phase particle IK solves the spine first (one length constraint between branch points "no matter how many spine bodies exist between"), then limbs. — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- **Skeleton-Aware Networks (Aberman et al., SIGGRAPH 2020)**: "different homeomorphic skeletons may be reduced to a common **primal skeleton** by a sequence of edge merging operations" (skeletal pooling); limitation — only homeomorphic skeletons (same branching structure). — [arXiv 2005.05732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05732)

### Inferences
- The consensus correspondence primitive is **(chain id, t ∈ [0,1])** plus landmark joints at chain ends (hip/shoulder/root, foot/hand/head/tail tip). A bone graph of 10–40 nodes can be mapped this way if every node is assigned to a chain and a parameter along it; helper nodes (muscle bulges) attach to a chain at some t but are not correspondence points.
- "Homeomorphic reduction" (SAN) = merge degree-2 nodes along chains → the primal skeleton is just the branching structure. Biped vs quadruped vs snake differ in *which chains exist*; the shared part (spine/neck/head/tail) is homeomorphic, limbs are present/absent. Correspondence for absent chains must be defined by a rule (collapse to attachment point, or grow from it), as Spore does with pseudo-feet.
- Normalizing by chain length (Unreal Interpolated, Spore LimbLength) is exactly what makes proportion changes cheap: interpolate chain lengths and per-t thickness, keep topology.

### Gaps
- Unreal/KineFX docs describe *animation* retargeting (poses), not *shape* morphing; their use for morphing rest-pose silhouettes between species is my inference, not documented practice.

## Q3. Shipped games/tools that morph a creature between species or stages — technique and cost

### Takeaway
Shipped games mostly avoid true continuous cross-topology morphing. Techniques in order of frequency: **mesh/model swap** masked by an effect or cut (werewolf/evolution cutscenes — common practice, poorly documented), **blend shapes + bone re-positioning within one skeleton** (Daz, UE Mesh Morpher werewolf demo), **parametric distortion of a single archetype** (No Man's Sky, SMAL/Reveret), and **procedural rigs over arbitrary graphs** (Spore, Rain World) where "morphing" is just rebuilding the body and letting procedural animation adapt.

### Cited Findings
- UE5 + MetaHuman procedural werewolf transformation (Elvis Morelli, reported 2026): **4 blend shapes for transformation phases + teeth morph**; "bone snapshots" via the Mesh Morpher plugin "to re adapt the bones to the new geometry", because blend shapes alone leave the skeleton mismatched; plus shader/groom/texture changes per stage. A demo, not a shipped game. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/watch-this-fantastic-procedural-werewolf-transformation-in-unreal-engine)
- Daz: morph + joint-center adjustment linked to the same slider (ERC Freeze) — the standard offline way to keep the rig valid when a shape morph changes proportions. — [Daz docs](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/software/dazstudio/4/userguide/creating_content/modeling/tutorials/auto_rig/start)
- No Man's Sky: blueprint + parameters — "as height changes, weight can change, the proportions… the animations can change, the bones can get bigger, thicker" (interview-level). — [Rambus](https://www.rambus.com/blogs/the-algorithms-of-no-mans-sky-2/)
- Spore: creature edited live; "the in-game editor begins playing animations on the character as soon as the player adds feet, mouths, or graspers". Cost: ~0.2 ms/frame for a 25-body creature on a 1.7 GHz Pentium-M (~35% in IK); validation by testers on a database of uploaded creatures, ~90% pass rate over several hundred creatures × ~1000 animations. — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Rain World (GDC 2016, Jakobsson & Therrien): creatures are a few physics "body chunks" (slugcat = two circles at fixed distance), limbs/tails drawn and animated procedurally on top — same approach for all creatures. — [Game Developer: Animating Rain World](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/video-animating-i-rain-world-i-and-its-many-squishy-stretchy-creatures); [GameAnim](https://www.gameanim.com/2017/07/24/rain-world-animation-process/)

### Inferences
- For a stylized game with **discrete stages**, the cheap, proven pattern is: per stage, a target *parameter vector* on the creature's own topology (chain lengths, per-t thickness, posture angles), with the rig moved together with the shape (Daz principle). Continuous blending between stages is then interpolation of that vector; no cross-topology geometry blending needed.
- The "donor's proportions" have to be *transferred through the correspondence* (chain,t) onto the carrier's own graph; that transfer is exactly the missing piece noted in the brief.

### Gaps
- Found no primary technical postmortem of a shipped game doing continuous species-to-species skeleton interpolation at runtime (searched werewolf/evolution/mutation). Evolution sims (Species: ALRE, Thrive, Planetary Life) have no technical docs found in this pass.

## Q4. Lessons, pitfalls, recommendations for runtime procedural bodies

### Takeaway
Explicit human-authored semantics beat inferred ones; chain-normalized parameters make proportion changes robust; keep helper bones out of correspondence; test generalization against a matrix of extreme bodies; expect a long tail of failures on unusual morphologies.

### Cited Findings
- Spore: "Although there is an appeal to algorithmically discovering semantics, we find having a human simply tell the system what is important is immensely powerful, efficient, and robust." — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Spore limitations: no notion of volume/boundaries (cannot express "hug", "rub chin"), no intra-character collision, IK singularities cause twisting; animators need "weeks to build up an intuition about which kinds of motions generalize"; "multiple equivalent ways to author an animation on a single character that specialize completely differently onto other characters". Gait system kept separate from the generalization system "for historical reasons". — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Spore testing: validation grid (creature × animation) with testers, stochastic sampling from a live creature database. — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)
- Mecanim: quality degrades gracefully with fewer mapped optional bones — required core + optional detail. — [Unity Manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/Retargeting.html)
- Blend shapes alone break bone alignment when proportions change; the rig must be re-fit per stage (werewolf demo; Daz ERC). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/watch-this-fantastic-procedural-werewolf-transformation-in-unreal-engine); [Versluis 2021](https://www.versluis.com/2021/02/daz-erc-freeze/)
- Homeomorphism requirement in learned retargeting — correspondence across different branching needs explicit rules. — [Aberman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05732)
- Few parameters explain most of the variation within one body plan (Reveret: 3 PCA components ≈ 90% of quadruped skeleton variance). — [Reveret et al. 2005](http://morpheo.inrialpes.fr/people/reveret/MorphableSkeleton/index.html)

### Inferences (recommendations for a small-graph runtime system — my synthesis)
1. Add a **semantic layer on nodes**, not a shared skeleton: each node gets `chain` (spine, neck, head, tail, limb_front_L/R, limb_hind_L/R, …) and `t ∈ [0,1]` along it, plus a small tag set (Spore-style caps: root, hip/shoulder attachment, foot, head tip). Derive hips/shoulders by graph walk, as Spore does.
2. Correspondence between species = per shared chain, compare functions of t (length share, thickness profile, angle/posture). Helper/bulge nodes ride on a chain at their t and are not matched.
3. Missing chains need an explicit rule (e.g. snake has no limb chains → a limb target collapses to the attachment point; legless locomotion uses spine as pseudo-feet, Spore precedent).
4. Morph by interpolating **chain-level parameters in normalized space**, then rebuild the rig from them (rig follows shape — Daz principle), rather than blending vertices or bone lists.
5. Build a matrix test (every carrier × every donor × every stage), Spore-style validation grid — cheap given 5 species.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies runtime cost of rebuilding a procedural body per stage in modern engines; Spore's 2008 number is the only hard figure found.
